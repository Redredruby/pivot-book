About the book

How much of who we think we are comes from the ways we have learned to respond? We withdraw when hurt, defend ourselves when criticized, or set aside our needs to avoid disappointing someone. In time, these familiar emotional reactions can become an account of ourselves that we rarely question. PIVOT: The Art of Moving Beyond Our Familiar Selves opens that account to further discovery.

Drawing on her work as a clinician, psychological research, and contemplative and spiritual perspectives, Rebecca A. Tavangar offers five movements of attention: Pause, Identify, Validate, Orient, and Transcend. Through them, we learn to recognize and understand what we feel while becoming more available to the people, circumstances, and possibilities before us. We may find ourselves able to speak honestly where we have always kept the peace, receive care when we are accustomed to managing alone, or pursue an interest we had assumed was beyond us.

Written for readers who recognize their reactions yet struggle to respond differently, PIVOT offers stories, reflection, and brief practices rooted in ordinary life. Its invitation is to approach ourselves with enough understanding to recognize a choice, and enough curiosity to discover where that choice might lead. What begins in a difficult moment can gradually change our sense of who we are and who we might become.

The PIVOT Method

The method arranges established findings from neuroscience into a sequence that can be learned and repeated. Each step builds on the one before it, moving from the body's first response toward a wider and more grounded view of what is happening.

  1. P

    Pause

    Interrupt the momentum of reaction long enough for the body to register what is happening, opening a small space between the feeling and the response.

  2. I

    Identify

    Name what is arising, both as sensation in the body and in words, with enough precision that the feeling becomes something you can observe and understand.

  3. V

    Validate

    Recognize the feeling as a meaningful signal from a nervous system doing its work, and meet it with acceptance and compassion.

  4. O

    Orient

    Turn attention toward the present moment and your surroundings, helping the nervous system register safety and settle into a steadier state.

  5. T

    Transcend

    Move beyond the immediate reaction toward a broader perspective, where the feeling can be held within a larger sense of meaning and purpose.

About the author
