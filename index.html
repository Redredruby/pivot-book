About the book

How much of who we think we are comes from the ways we have learned to respond? We withdraw when hurt, defend ourselves when criticized, or put our needs aside to avoid disappointing someone. Over time, familiar emotional reactions can begin to feel like the limits of who we are. PIVOT: The Art of Moving Beyond Our Familiar Selves invites us to look again.

Drawing on clinical experience, psychological research, and contemplative and spiritual perspectives, Rebecca A. Tavangar explores how attention can help us understand what we feel and recognize choices we had overlooked. Speaking honestly, allowing someone to care for us, or pursuing a neglected interest can reveal capacities we scarcely knew were ours. Giving those capacities expression helps them grow.

For readers who recognize their reactions yet struggle to respond differently, PIVOT offers stories, reflection, and brief practices grounded in ordinary life. Its hope reaches beyond a single response. What we discover in a difficult moment can change our understanding of ourselves and what might still become possible.

The PIVOT Method

PIVOT brings together five movements of attention. They help us recognize an emotional response, understand it with compassion, and take in more of the situation we are living. The movements can overlap, and we can begin with whichever is available in the moment.

  1. P

    Pause

    Allow a moment to notice what is happening before the next action. A breath, an unfinished reply, or the recognition that you have reached your limit can offer a place to begin.

  2. I

    Identify

    Find words for what you feel. Notice the bodily sensations and thoughts that accompany it, allowing the experience to become more specific as you attend.

  3. V

    Validate

    Give the feeling room to be understood. Consider how your history, present circumstances, and what matters to you may help explain why it is here.

  4. O

    Orient

    Take in more of the whole moment. Your body, emotions, and thoughts belong alongside the people and circumstances around you. What you notice may reveal a need, available support, or something your first reaction left out.

  5. T

    Transcend

    Consider what you want to make room for, even while difficulty remains. A relationship, a responsibility, or something you love may open another way to respond. Through that choice, you may discover more about yourself.

About the author

Rebecca A. Tavangar is a licensed professional counselor who works with people living with trauma and deeply established emotional patterns. She is interested in what happens when someone begins to understand a response they have spent years judging or trying to change. In her work, she listens closely to the words a person uses, the experience they are struggling to describe, and the circumstances that make their feelings understandable.

Her approach is patient and curious. Alongside a person’s history, she remains interested in capacities that may have had little opportunity to emerge. A lifelong spiritual inquiry, together with her study of contemplative psychology, has deepened that interest in what remains possible within a human life. As a mother, musician, and clinician, she also knows how understanding can be difficult to reach when we need it most. PIVOT grew from this continuing inquiry, in her work and her own life, into how we can meet ourselves with compassion and discover more freedom in the way we live.
